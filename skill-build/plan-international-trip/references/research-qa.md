# Research and feasibility QA

Use this checklist for substantive trip planning. Select only sources relevant to the destination, but complete every applicable check.

## Intake and approval gates

- Record the destination, dates or day count, flight status, lodging status, traveler count, fixed wishes, transport preference, and walking tolerance before finalizing the route.
- Skip questions the traveler already answered and distinguish fixed choices from items the assistant may arrange.
- Do not build the website before the traveler approves the itinerary preview.
- Keep booking and recheck dates provisional in the preview; finalize the checklist and calendar events only after itinerary approval.
- Do not publish before the traveler approves the local website and its visibility.

## Source hierarchy

| Topic | Primary evidence | Useful secondary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flights and airports | Dated e-ticket; airline and airport pages | Flight trackers for disruption context only |
| Rail, metro, bus, ferries | Operator timetable, fare, pass and service pages | Route planners and maps for cross-checking |
| Holidays and entry rules | Government and embassy sources | Reputable travel advisories |
| Attractions and events | Venue or organizer page | Tourism board |
| Restaurants | Official page or booking system | Native ranking site, local guides, map reviews |
| Lodging | Property confirmation and official listing | Map, neighborhood and recent review evidence |
| Crafts and specialty goods | Maker catalog, origin label, craft association | Established retailer |

Do not treat a search-result snippet, influencer list, or unsourced travel blog as final authority when a primary source exists.

## Calendar and routing checks

- Recalculate every date and weekday; check national and local holidays.
- Confirm station names precisely, including similarly named stations and the correct operator.
- Check whether routes require reserved seats, supplements, airport-car orientation, or ticket activation.
- Include realistic station walking, transfers, queues, meals, winter daylight, and 20–30 minute crowd buffers where appropriate.
- Check last admission separately from closing time.
- Avoid scheduling a venue on its regular or exceptional closure day.
- Keep arrival day flexible and reach the airport according to the carrier’s check-in rule.
- Give a disruption fallback for the airport and any critical reservation.

## Food and shopping checks

- Record rating source, ranking scope, score, and research date; expect scores to move.
- Verify opening hours, holidays, reservation window, lateness rule, price range, payment, and whether the desired item can sell out.
- Give one feasible primary and one nearby or lower-friction fallback for each must-eat category.
- Do not call a restaurant “local #1” unless a current ranking page supports that exact scope.
- For crafts, distinguish “made in the country” from “sold in the country.” Verify origin label, maker, model, and store inventory separately.
- Provide a short local-language stock or reservation phrase when it materially helps.

## Lodging checks

- Confirm the exact property and nearest useful station, not a similarly named branch.
- Evaluate airport and intercity access, daily transit friction, groceries, kitchen/laundry, room noise, luggage storage, staffed hours, cancellation, and total price.
- Recommend changing a booked or favored property only when the alternative produces a clear benefit after price and moving costs.

## Fare checks

- List planned paid rides covered by each candidate pass and estimate their single fares.
- Account for consecutive-day requirements, excluded operators, seat supplements, QR or internet dependency, purchase channels, activation rules, and refund constraints.
- Do not stack passes without showing why both recover their cost.
- State when contactless cards, stored-value transit cards, or individual tickets are the simplest and cheapest answer.

## Final consistency pass

- No two fixed bookings overlap.
- Each day has a meal window and a feasible route home.
- Restaurant and attraction hours match arrival time.
- Warnings are visible where the traveler needs them, not buried in notes.
- Costs are labeled per person or per party and use a stated currency.
- Claims about future availability are provisional and include a recheck date.
- All essential links open the exact venue, product, reservation page, or route target.

## Website, calendar, and publication checks

- Match the site’s visual direction to the destination while preserving readable contrast, keyboard focus, touch targets, and mobile layout.
- Place meal and route backups beside the primary choice where the traveler needs them.
- Put every advance restaurant booking, timed ticket, inventory hold, business-hour recheck, and final flight check in the pre-departure checklist.
- Validate each Google Calendar template link: encoded title, dates, timezone, details, location, and exact official action link.
- Use the traveler’s home timezone for pre-departure tasks and the destination timezone for on-trip events.
- Explain that public calendar links create personal copies and do not grant the website calendar access.
- Remove booking references, barcodes, passport details, phone numbers, auth tokens, and other secrets before publishing.
- Confirm the traveler understands that public content may be found by anyone or a search engine, while companions have viewing, not repository write, access.
- Verify the production URL loads the current assets and key itinerary content after deployment.
